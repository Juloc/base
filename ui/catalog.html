How to use the kit

Three files in the head, nothing to build (CODE-06). The app sets its colour with one variable in its own stylesheet, a colour or a hue (COLOR-01) and builds its pages from the parts below. Everything is served under /base/ by the base itself.

<script src="/base/boot.js"></script>
<link rel="stylesheet" href="/base/base.css">
<link rel="stylesheet" href="/app.css">        /* :root { --accent-color: #00e5ff; } or { --accent-hue: 152; } */
<script type="module" src="/app.js"></script>

// app.js
import { h, icon, api, shell, router, toast, openDialog } from '/base/base.js';
import { t, addMessages } from '/base/i18n.js';
import { start } from '/base/auth.js';      // setup assistant and sign-in, then your app

The look and where it comes from

The look of the family is that of JulOS, its main app: Material 3 Expressive built from one seed colour (m3.css there). The kit takes its roles, tone steps, radii, type scale and motion and keeps its own token names; the one seed colour is --accent-hue. The default hue (299) is the hue of the JulOS seed, so an app that sets none looks like JulOS. Since 0.3.1 the dark face and the capsules, chips and floating bar also take from Jularr (blue-black tinted by the hue, fine edges instead of shadows).

Where the kit differs on purpose: labels stay above the fields instead of floating inside (FORM-01, no script); a dialog never darkens or blocks the page (DIALOG-01); body text is 16 px; the colours of success and warning are the kit's own. Where a person asks for reduced transparency the glass is the solid surface; in forced colours edges and outlines take over.

Sheet

Colours

Only these tokens; never a colour value in an app (COLOR-01). An app names a colour (--accent-color: #00e5ff) or only a hue (--accent-hue: 152); pick either above and the whole page follows. The fill (--accent) is the colour itself and is for switches that are on, the brand mark and charts; links, icons, lines and rings use --accent-ink; the selection (current page, chosen segment, primary button) is soft (--accent-soft with --on-accent-soft). Faults are raspberry, warnings soft amber.

Button

One primary button per view. The label says what happens ("Delete entry", not "OK"). A finger needs 44 px (MOBILE-01); the height comes from --control-h. Text is bold and the radius is 14 px; a button keeps its shape and colour when pressed. The primary button is the soft accent (the same surface as the current menu entry); the bright accent is for switches, rings and charts.

Row button (mini)

The small button in a row of a list or a table: .btn.mini, 34 px high, radius 11, bold. Tonal (soft accent) by default, .strong is the accent fill, .quiet has no surface, .danger is raspberry; .icon-only for a symbol. The target stays 44 px for a finger.

Field

Every control has a visible label (FORM-01), short and above the field; what the field wants goes into its placeholder, a hint below explains more, the error replaces guessing (FORM-02). The input is 48 px high with a faint edge, a soft shadow and a soft ring in the ink of the accent when focused. In JavaScript: field({ label, name, placeholder, hint }), passwordField().

Lower-case letters, digits, dot, dash and underscore.

The password needs at least 12 characters.

Dropdown

Our own instead of the browser's select: field({ type: 'select', options }) or dropdown() alone. The list is styled, the chosen entry tinted with a check. With search (on by default above ten entries) a field at the top filters as one types. Keyboard: Enter, Space and the arrows open; arrows, Home, End and PageUp/PageDown move; Enter chooses; Escape closes; typing jumps to a match. Screen readers get a combobox with a listbox and aria-activedescendant. On a phone the list is a sheet from below, on a wide screen it hangs at the button and fits the window; it closes on a click outside and when the page changes. A form reads the value as before (formValues): a hidden input with the name carries it and fires change.

The browser's own select remains for markup written by hand (the class select, the same look); prefer dropdown().

Switch, checkbox, segmented choice, choice tiles

A switch (46 × 28, a white knob, the accent when on) applies at once; a checkbox (in the ink of the accent) waits for "Save". Two to four short options side by side: the segmented choice, the chosen part white on a light track. Two to four options that each need a sentence: choiceTiles(), the chosen tile tinted with an ink border (radio buttons inside, so the arrow keys work).

Capsule and chip

A capsule holds a group in one round outline, the chosen part is a soft accent pill inside it (from Jularr), as the current menu entry and the chosen tab. segmented() makes one for choices; capsule({ label, items }) makes one for places, for example in the head of a page. A chip is a rounded rectangle with a fine edge for categories and filters; it can be a button (aria-pressed), a link or plain text.

Page head and bars

The head of a page is made by shell() from the view: { title, sub, actions } (big title, a second line, the main action at the right; back adds the way back). A place of nav with foot: true goes into a second group at the bottom of the side list.

For what waits for a decision ("Apply changes (3)") the action bar: a white card centred above the menu on a phone, at the bottom of the content on a wide screen, one or two buttons, update() changes its words in place. floatBar() is the old name of the same bar. While rows are selected the dark selection bar takes its place: "3 selected", the actions that work on them and a button to clear the selection.

Table() and the tree

table({ label, columns, rows | groups, actions, active, detail, selection }) (/base/parts.js) makes a white table: a small grey head, 50 px rows, one action column at the right (at most one tonal text button, then quiet icon buttons; the switch is always last). Wide it is a real table; on a phone every row has two lines, the switch or the main action stays at the right and the other actions move into a menu. A row folds open to a detail (several at once); rows with children fold as a tree with fine lines; groups have a head with the count, +, ⋮ and a switch that greys every row below. Check boxes drive the selection bar; on a phone press "Select" or hold a row.

Tabs and chips

tabs() is a white bar with the chosen tab soft (for switching what a page shows, also for periods with size: 's'); capsule() has the same look for links to places; chips() is a row of filters. On a phone each is one row you swipe sideways, with a soft edge where there is more.

Number box

numberBox({ icon, value, label, change }): a tonal icon square, a big number, a small label, an optional change (an arrow and a text). numberStrip() lays them in a grid; on a phone it is a strip you swipe. ring(value, max, state) is a ring with a number for days left.

Chart

chart({ title, series, data }): a stacked area chart in SVG without a library. Smooth layers with a thin line in the colour of the card between them, three faint guide lines, the axis below. Click a name in the legend to hide that series; hover (or tap, or use the arrow keys) for a dark box with every value. The colours are given by the app, any CSS colour. A sentence that sums the chart up is there for a screen reader.

Live feed

liveFeed({ title, max }): rows arrive at the top with add() without anything being rebuilt, each with a colour dot; the button pauses it (the rows wait and are counted) and resumes it. No toast for such events.

Timeline

timeline({ days }): day headings, cards on a line, each with a round node that is green, amber or raspberry (and says it in words for a screen reader).

Folding card

foldCard({ title, sub, lead, actions, body }): a card whose head opens its body (the body can be made on first opening). The head is a button with aria-expanded; actions sit beside it.

Event in place

flash(element): the row or card lights up briefly in the soft accent. Use it for what happens on the page (a new request, a renewed certificate) instead of a toast; toasts stay for "saved" and the failure of an action. With reduced motion it is a still tint that goes away.

Toast

A short confirmation that goes away by itself. Never the only place for something the person must not miss; that is a notice.

Dialog

Big and centred on a wide screen (720 px; size: 'l' up to 940 px, size: 's' 480 px for a question), a sheet from below on a phone with its buttons fixed at the bottom. Solid, never see-through, a faint scrim behind. It does not block the page and can be moved by its title (DIALOG-01), yet only one is open at a time: opened from the page it replaces the open one, the same button pressed again only brings that one forward, and a question asked from inside a dialog stands above it; Escape closes it and the focus returns. It closes when the page changes (the router calls closeDialogs()). Buttons: cancel and the main action bottom right, delete and deactivate bottom left with side: 'start'. Two columns: a .dialog-cols with a .dialog-col in each column. Before something that cannot be undone, ask, and name the action on the button (DIALOG-01).

Menu

A few actions at a button. Arrow keys move, Escape closes.

Form with errors

submit(form, fn) makes the button busy and shows what the server refused at the field it belongs to; the first wrong field gets the focus (FORM-02). Send it empty to see.

People and the central sign-in

For the settings page of the role admin: peopleCard(me) of people.js (the list, an invitation link per new person, change the role, block, delete) and hubCard(me) of hub.js (address, client id, secret, on or off, check the connection). roleName(me, key) gives the name of a role as the app named it. The sign-in page of auth.js shows the button of the centre below its own form, and the invitation page (#invite=...) takes a new person through password and authenticator.

Sign-in card

authCard({ icon, title, line, target?, step?, body, foot }) (from /base/gate.js): the door of every app is one white card in the middle of the page (radius 28, soft shadow; on a phone it fills the width with a 16 px margin): the mark of the app, a title, one line, then what the page needs, and language and light or dark as quiet text buttons at the bottom. The sign-in, the first setup (with step: { n: 1, of: 3 }) and the invitation of the base are made with it. An app with pages of its own around a sign-in (a gate in front of other sites) uses the same call; target: { host, note? } adds the soft box "you go straight there afterwards". Fields on the card have their label for the screen reader only (labelHidden) and say what they want in the placeholder; the main button is the soft one, the hub button is .btn.neutral. mountAuth(card) puts the card on an empty page.

State card

stateCard({ icon, tone, title, text, actions }): the same card for one state, with a round mark, one sentence and one or two buttons. tone is 'run' (the mark turns, "you are being sent on"), 'ok' or 'danger' ("not allowed", "link expired"). The first action is the main one, a second is quiet; an action has href or run.

Offline line and new version

pwa() (from /base/base.js, called once at the start of an app) registers the service worker and keeps two quiet lines at the top of the window: offline when the device has no connection or a request did not get through (the shell still loads from the cache, the data says it is offline in words), and new version, reload when a new worker waits (nothing is replaced behind the person's back). They hang on the root element, so a change of the page does not remove them. The base serves the manifest, the icons (SVG and PNG, from the app's name, icon and colour in BaseAppOptions) and the worker; see the README.
